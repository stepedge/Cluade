# SA Market Conditions for Small Online IT Resellers & SME IT/Web/Hosting Providers (2025–2026)

Research date: 17 October 2026. Method note: WebFetch could not reach most SA domains in this session (DNS failures), so findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages. Figures are labelled by date. Anything tagged "(vendor)" is from a company that sells the product, so it may be biased.

## 1. Macro: consumer and SME spending, interest rates, e-commerce growth

### Takeaway
In mid/late 2026 the macro backdrop for discretionary and SME IT spend is weak and getting worse. GDP shrank in Q2 2026, business confidence is at a 2-year low (38), consumer confidence is negative (-13), and the SARB has **raised** rates twice since May 2026 (repo now 7.25%). Online retail is the exception: it is still growing ~22% a year, but that growth goes overwhelmingly to large platforms.

### Cited Findings
**Interest rates (2026 tightening cycle)**
- SARB held the repo at 6.75% in Feb 2026. It then hiked 25bp to 7.00% on 28 May 2026, its first hike since 2023. It held at 7.00% on 23 Jul 2026 (prime 10.50%) on a 4–2 split vote, with 2 members wanting a hike. It hiked again 25bp to **7.25% on 23 Sep 2026** (unanimous), putting prime at about 10.75%. CPI was 4.4% in Aug 2026. — [EWN, Jul 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/07/23/sarb-keeps-interest-rates-unchanged-surprising-markets-and-analysts); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/interest-rate/news/586122); [FocusEconomics](https://www.focus-economics.com/countries/south-africa/news/monetary-policy/south-africa-central-bank-meeting-23-07-2026-south-african-reserve-bank-holds-fire-in-july/)
- Implication: SME credit and overdraft costs are rising, and so are consumer instalment costs (laptops on credit, BNPL).

**GDP / labour**
- GDP **-0.2% q/q in Q2 2026**, ending six quarters of growth. It was +0.9% y/y, and Q1 2026 was +0.4% q/q. Trade fell 1.9%, manufacturing 1.8% and mining 3.0%, and investment declined for a second consecutive quarter. Household consumption still rose. — [SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/84138); [EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/08/sa-economy-shrinks-in-q2-after-six-quarters-of-growth); [Stats SA P0441 Q2 2026](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0441/Press%20release%20-%20Q2%202026.pdf)
- Official unemployment rose to 33.6%, per an IOL summary relayed in search results (secondary; not verified against the QLFS).

**Business confidence (RMB/BER BCI)**
- Q1 2026: **47**, a near-5-year high. Q2 2026: **39**, an 8-point drop. Q3 2026: **38**, the lowest since Q3 2024. 62% of respondents are dissatisfied with business conditions. Manufacturing fell 31→27. The drivers cited are the Middle East conflict and "faltering domestic demand". The local government elections on 4 Nov 2026 are flagged as a Q4 risk. — [Engineering News / RMB-BER, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/print-version/rmbber-business-confidence-index-stabilises-after-second-quarter-setback-2026-09-02); [IOL, Mar 2026](https://iol.co.za/business-report/companies/2026-03-04-business-confidence-climbs-to-near-5-year-high-but-geopolitical-tensions-pose-risks/)

**Consumer confidence (FNB/BER CCI)**
- Q2 2026: **-19**, the lowest since Q1 2025. Q3 2026: **-13**. Lower-income households drove the improvement. Wealthier consumers (the main buyers of laptops and IT gear) "remain deeply pessimistic", and willingness to make major purchases is declining. FNB expects cost-conscious festive shoppers. — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/consumer-confidence/news/584526); [AllAfrica, Sep 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202609180186.html); [BusinessDay TV](https://www.businessday.co.za/bdtv/2026-09-17-watch-what-south-africas-latest-consumer-confidence-index-tells-us/)

**Retail sales (Stats SA P6242.1, y/y real)**
- Jan 2026 +4.2%; Mar +2.6%; Apr ~+1.2% (revised); May +2.3% (later 2.2%); Jun +1.6% first print, revised to 1.1%; Q2 2026 +1.7% vs Q2 2025; **Jul 2026 +3.4%** (consensus 0.9%). — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/retail-sales-annual/news/584217); [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/sa-retail-sales-rise-16-in-june-as-other-retailers-and-general-dealers-lead-growth-745502a); [Stats SA May 2026](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P62421/P62421May2026.pdf)
- Nov 2025 retail sales were +3.5% y/y, helped by Black Friday. — [IOL Business Report, Jan 2026](https://iol.co.za/business-report/economy/2026-01-25-retail-sales-climb-35-in-november-as-black-friday-spurs-spending/). A Feb 2026 IOL headline referred to "disappointing December activity" and modest 2026 growth expectations. — [IOL, Feb 2026](https://iol.co.za/business-report/companies/2026-02-19-retail-sales-expected-to-grow-modestly-in-2026-after-disappointing-december-activity/)

**Online retail (World Wide Worx / Mastercard / Peach Payments)**
- 2024: R96bn (+35%), 8% of retail. 2025: ~R130bn (+35.4%), 8.49% of retail turnover by end-2025. **2026 forecast: R159bn (+22.5%)**, with growth slowing. Online passed 10% of retail in mid-2026 for the first time. — [World Wide Worx 2026](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2026/); [ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/sa-online-retail-races-to-r159bn-as-growth-accelerates/o1Jr5MxPopQMKdWL); [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/online-retail-south-africa-2026-world-wide-worx/285678/); [IOL](https://iol.co.za/business/2026-09-03-sa-online-shopping-hits-record-r159bn-as-e-commerce-reaches-10-of-retail/); [WWW 2025](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2025/)
- The share of the population shopping online **fell** from 36.6% (2024) to 34.2% (2025), so growth comes from existing shoppers spending more, not from new buyers. — [World Wide Worx 2026 via search summary](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2026/)
- Big retailers' online growth: Checkers Sixty60 +34.5% to R25.5bn (FY to Jun 2026), Pick n Pay online +32.7%, TFG Africa online +49.2%. — [Freight News](https://www.freightnews.co.za/article/sa-e-commerce-to-hit-r159bn-record)

### Inferences
- The macro picture supports a **market-driven headwind** for a small IT seller: weaker Q2–Q3 demand, two rate hikes, depressed high-income consumers and businesses holding back investment (GDP investment fell two quarters running). A slowdown in demand is likely. A complete stop in closed sales for several weeks, however, goes beyond what these numbers predict, because overall retail still grew 1–3% y/y and online grew more than 20%. That points to company-specific factors as well (traffic, visibility, price competitiveness, trust).
- Online growth is concentrated in large platforms. A static or shrinking pool of online shoppers means small sites must take buyers from incumbents rather than win new ones.

## 2. Competitive landscape: platforms, specialist e-tailers, distributors

### Takeaway
Takealot dominates SA online usage. Amazon.co.za is scaling fast after launching Prime in June 2026 (R59/month, free same/next-day delivery). IT specialists Evetech and Wootware dominate component and PC e-commerce, while Incredible leads in-store PC/laptop purchases. A small generalist IT web store competes against all of them on price, range and delivery speed, and is unlikely to win on any of the three.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 World Wide Worx usage: **Takealot 35.3%** of online shoppers, Shein 21.5%, Checkers Sixty60 15%, Amazon 12.7% (measured before Prime launched). By value: Takealot.com GMV is ~R30bn vs Shein+Temu ~R7.5bn. Industry estimates put Takealot at ~24% of SA online retail revenue. — [BusinessDay, Jul 2026](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-07-09-takealot-dominates-sa-ecommerce-as-shoppers-favour-chinese-imports/); [MyBroadband](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/business/629592-takealot-beats-amazon-shein-and-temu.html); [ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/takealot-extends-e-commerce-lead-despite-arrival-of-disruptors/rW1xLv5n3Z17Rk6m)
- Takealot Group FY2026 revenue was R17.7bn (+18%), with its first full-year aEBIT profit of R171m. — via search summary of [MyBroadband/ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/takealot-extends-e-commerce-lead-despite-arrival-of-disruptors/rW1xLv5n3Z17Rk6m) (secondary)
- **Amazon Prime SA launched 3 Jun 2026**: R59/month or R399/year, unlimited same/next-day delivery with no minimum spend in major cities. The first SA Prime Day ran 23–29 Jun 2026. Amazon uses local courier partners and low seller fees. — [EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/06/09/amazon-prime-launches-in-south-africa-with-streaming-and-free-delivery-for-r59-a-month); [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/amazon-launches-prime-in-south-africa-with-fast-free-delivery-exclusive-deals-and-streaming-entertainment-in-a-single-membership-2026-06-03); [BusinessDay](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-06-03-amazon-launches-prime-in-sa-as-e-commerce-battle-intensifies/)
- Trust favours local platforms: 46% of South Africans have more confidence in local platforms vs 10% in overseas ones (2025 report). — [Mastercard 2025](https://www.mastercard.com/news/eemea/en/newsroom/press-releases/en/2025-1/september/south-africa-s-online-retail-set-to-surpass-r130-billion-in-2025)
- A Hypertext survey (May 2026) found **Incredible at 19.6%** of most-recent PC/laptop purchases, second only to one leading retailer (unnamed in the snippet, probably Takealot) and ahead of Evetech and Wootware. — [Hypertext, May 2026](https://htxt.co.za/2026/05/one-retailer-leads-the-way-when-south-africans-buy-pcs-and-laptops/) (full article not accessible; the leader's share is unconfirmed)
- Evetech calls itself SA's largest online hardware retailer. In April 2026 it reported strong gaming-PC demand but a shift away from high-end parts because of memory costs. Wootware reports fast-growing demand for AI workstations. — [MyBroadband, Apr 2026](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/hardware/642437-most-popular-pc-sold-online-in-south-africa.html)
- **2026 memory/RAM price shock**: AI and data-centre demand is absorbing global DRAM supply. Higher prices were expected to reach SA retail in May–June 2026 as existing stock ran out. An industry source calls it "structural", and entry-level R3,999–R4,999 laptops face steep increases. — [Daily Maverick, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-03-23-laptop-blues-sa-cant-hide-from-the-ram-price-spike). 16GB DDR5 kits cost R900–R1,300 and 32GB R1,400–R2,000 at Evetech (vendor). — [Evetech EveZone](https://evezone.evetech.co.za/quick-bytes/why-ram-prices-climbing-in-sa-in-2026-what-sa-gamers-should-know)

### Inferences
- With rising component and laptop prices and weak confidence, buyers delay purchases or compare harder. Small resellers that buy from distributors at list price cannot match Takealot, Amazon or Evetech on price. Where they do win, it is on service: install, configuration, CCTV design and fitting, on-site support, bundled SLAs, B2B credit terms and tender paperwork (B-BBEE Level 1).
- Amazon Prime's free next-day delivery since June 2026 raises what consumers expect from delivery. A small store that charges R100+ for courier and takes 3–5 days is now at a clear disadvantage for commodity items like printers, ink and peripherals. The timing (June 2026 onward) may partly coincide with the client's slump.
- Distributors (Rectron, Mustek, Tarsus, Axiz) sell to resellers, and some resellers list distributor catalogues directly. Many small IT sites run the same distributor catalogue feeds, so product listings are effectively interchangeable across sites.

## 3. E-commerce conversion, AOV, abandonment, trust factors

### Takeaway
SA cart abandonment is about 83% (2024, ECDB), above the ~75% global average. Online baskets are rising (Absa Black Friday 2025 online average R827). Trust depends on local presence, instant EFT and real-time payments, BNPL, fast delivery and reviews. Small unknown stores start at a trust disadvantage.

### Cited Findings
- SA add-to-cart rate 9.0–9.5% and **cart abandonment 83.0–83.5%** (2024), vs ~75% globally and 85.15% across Africa. — [ECDB](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/market/za/all); [Stitch, Mar 2026](https://stitch.money/blog/how-to-reduce-cart-abandonment-on-your-e-commerce-store-in-south-africa) (vendor)
- An indicative SA AOV of ~US$54.50 (27 Mar 2025) comes from a volatile Grips Intelligence daily series. — [CEIC](https://ceicdata.com/en/south-africa/ecommerce-transactions-by-device)
- Payment methods (2025 WWW report): PayShap/real-time bank payments are offered by 40% of retailers and BNPL by 16%. In 2024, cards (100%) and EFT (95%) were universal, and enterprise merchants used vouchers (61%), BNPL (47%) and payment links (44%). — [Peach Payments 2025 highlights](https://www.peachpayments.com/scale/world-wide-worx-2025-online-retail-report/) (vendor); [Peach 2024](https://quiet-orchard.peachpayments.com/scale/world-wide-worx-2024-online-retail-report)
- Black Friday 2025: Absa's average online basket was **R827 (+25% y/y)** vs in-store R496. Standard Bank card-not-present transactions were **22% of volume** (vs 12% in 2024). — [BusinessDay, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2025-12-03-online-purchase-growth-outpaces-physical-retail-on-black-friday-banks-say/)

### Inferences
- At a typical 1–2% e-commerce conversion rate (a general benchmark, not SA-sourced), a store with a few hundred to a thousand sessions a month would expect only a handful of orders. "Several weeks with no sales" can be **statistical noise at low traffic**. The client's own session count is the critical diagnostic.
- Trust gaps for small stores include an unknown brand, no Google or Hellopeter reviews, no instant EFT, BNPL or PayShap, unclear returns and warranty terms, and slow or expensive delivery. These are company-specific factors the client can fix.

### Gaps
- No reliable SA-specific conversion-rate or IT-category AOV benchmarks found.
- No 2026 data on PayJustNow, Ozow or PayFast adoption shares; the 2026 WWW payment section was not accessible.
- No SA data on delivery or returns concerns as purchase barriers found in this session.

## 4. SME demand and pricing for website design, hosting and managed IT

### Takeaway
Hosting and simple websites are commoditised in SA. Entry hosting costs R35–R99/month, and bottom-tier sites cost R1,500–R5,000. Mid-market SME sites are R8k–R20k, and B2B sites R20k–R75k. Small providers get squeezed between cheap global and local hosts or DIY builders at the bottom and agencies at the top.

### Cited Findings
- Entry shared hosting (2026 listings, mostly from vendor comparison sites): Afrihost ~R84–R89/month (2GB); Xneelo from ~R99/month (5GB), R149 for 10GB; Hostinger ~R69/month or US$2.49 intro, with higher renewal pricing (one example: $10.99/month) and the nearest data centre in Europe; Truehost from R35/month (vendor claim); HostAfrica from R99/month. — [Truehost comparison](https://truehost.co.za/best-web-hosting-south-africa-2026-comparison/) (vendor); [coupondeals](https://coupondeals.co.za/blog/hostafrica-vs-xneelo-vs-afrihost/); [Guru99](https://www.guru99.com/best-web-hosting-south-africa.html)
- Website design price bands (2026 vendor guides): basic template WordPress site R1,500–R5,000 (4–6 pages); most SMEs R8,000–R15,000 custom. — [sowebsites](https://sowebsites.co.za/blog/website-cost-south-africa-2026). Procompare: business site R7,500–R20,000, simple CMS site R3,800–R6,300, brochure R5k–R20k, SME lead-gen R20k–R60k. — [Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/articles/digital-marketing-seo-services/website-cost-south-africa). Johannesburg B2B security-company site ~R36k–R75k. — [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/private-security-company-website-cost-johannesburg-2026) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Hosting at R99 is a low-margin commodity. The client's hosting and web revenue depends on bundling (site + hosting + email + support retainer) and on relationships, not on inbound web sales.
- SMEs under margin pressure (BCI 38, GDP contraction, rate hikes) tend to defer website redesigns and discretionary IT projects. Break-fix and security support stay more resilient.

### Gaps

- No 2026 survey of SME IT budgets in SA found.

## 5. Public-sector and corporate procurement

### Takeaway
Public procurement in SA is in legal flux. A Constitutional Court ruling reportedly invalidated the Public Procurement Act 28 of 2024 in Sept 2026 (unverified, single source), and an Amendment Bill was introduced in Aug 2026. Municipalities pay very late: 81% of municipal invoices were outstanding beyond 90 days (Dec 2024). B-BBEE Level 1 gives the full preference points but cannot overcome slow, uncertain, late-paying public demand.

### Cited Findings
- Draft General Public Procurement Regulations were published 16 Apr 2026, with comments closing 15 Jul 2026. The Public Procurement Amendment Bill B22-2026 was introduced 18 Aug 2026. The tracker reports the **Constitutional Court struck down the Public Procurement Act 28 of 2024 on 17 Sep 2026** for inadequate public participation, which leaves the draft regulations without a legal basis until that is fixed. — [Accountability Tracker](https://accountability-tracker.org/anti-corruption-policy-monitor/public-procurement-act/); [Treasury draft regs](https://www.treasury.gov.za/public%20comments/ProcReg/Draft%20General%20Public%20Procurement%20Regulations%202026%20for%20consultation%20ito%20section%2063(3)%20of%20Act.pdf). **Caution: a confirming search found no other source for the Sept 2026 ruling. Verify before relying on it.**
- Background: in 2022 the ConCourt (Afribusiness) invalidated the 2017 PPPFA Regulations, leading to the Preferential Procurement Regulations 2022 (organs of state set their own specific goals within the 80/20 and 90/10 systems). — [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/south-africa-public-procurement-cast-into-uncertainty-by-constitutional-court-judgment/); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/confusion-over-south-african-public-procurement)
- Municipal late payment: **81.2% of municipal invoices outstanding beyond 90 days** as at end Dec 2024 (National Treasury reply, Apr 2025). City of Johannesburg's average payment time was 68–134 days, Nelson Mandela Bay 190 days and Mangaung 168 days (2024/25). The 30-day MFMA rule is widely breached. 84% of municipalities failed to pay within 30 days in 2020-21 (AGSA, older data). The Small Enterprise Ombud appointment was still pending with Parliament as of Jun 2026. — [PMG parliamentary questions](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/40050/); [PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/38400/); [The Outlier chart](https://theoutlier.co.za/charts/chart/76afbdc3-b8cd-42e5-aeec-76dec3c0c25c)

### Inferences
- Pre-election caution (local government elections on 4 Nov 2026) and the legal uncertainty around procurement reform probably slow municipal and provincial RFQs in Q3–Q4 2026. This is a market-driven factor for any firm relying on tenders.
- For a firm with limited working capital, municipal work carries severe cash-flow risk: expect payment in 90+ days. National and provincial departments and SOEs, along with corporate enterprise and supplier development (ESD) procurement, are better targets for using B-BBEE Level 1 status.

### Gaps
- No 2026 eTender portal or CSD RFQ volume statistics found.
- Not verified: whether the Sept 2026 ConCourt ruling exists, and what transitional arrangements (PPPFA + PPR 2022 still applying?) are in force.
- No data on corporate B-BBEE-driven ICT procurement trends in 2026.

## 6. Seasonality: Q4, Black Friday, year-end budgets

### Takeaway
SA consumer IT demand is heavily concentrated in late November. Electronics were ~40% of Black Friday spend (2024). Many buyers postpone purchases in October and early November to wait for deals, so a pre-Black Friday lull is normal and partly explains a slump in October.

### Cited Findings
- Black Friday 2025: Nedbank cards recorded a record R2.84bn over the weekend (+4.5% y/y), Absa R5.5bn and FNB R3.4bn on the day. Four banks topped R30bn in 2024. Ecentric and Peach Payments settled more than R500m and ~1.5m transactions by noon on the day. — [BusinessDay / Sunday Times, Dec 2025](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/business/2025-12-13-bt1412-p1-splash-tills-ring-merrily-as-festive-fever-rises/); [Daily News](https://dailynews.co.za/business/economy/2025-11-28-black-friday-madness-south-africans-swipe-over-r500m-by-noon/)
- Absa: Black Friday 2025 card spend was only +1.4% y/y, but Cyber Monday was +11%. Card-not-present share was 22% vs 12% (Standard Bank). — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/black-friday-2025-set-to-break-records-as-south-african-retail-surges-174576a); [BusinessDay](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2025-12-03-online-purchase-growth-outpaces-physical-retail-on-black-friday-banks-say/)
- Electronics were ~40% of Black Friday spend (PayU, **2024 data**). — via search summary of [Zawya/Black Friday guide](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/the-ultimate-black-friday-2025-survival-guide-for-south-african-e-commerce-retailers-october-2025-r23269we) (secondary)
- Stats SA: November's share of combined Nov+Dec retail sales rose to 45.9% in 2025 (from 44.6% in 2023), so Black Friday is pulling spend into November. — [Stats SA "Black Friday effect"](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19300)

### Inferences
- October is typically a pre-Black Friday trough for consumer electronics, as shoppers hold off. The client's slump (if it covers Sep–Oct 2026) partly overlaps this seasonal pattern, plus the Sept rate hike and weak consumer confidence.
- B2B year-end: the SA government fiscal year ends 31 March, so public "use-it-or-lose-it" spend peaks in Feb–Mar, not Q4. Many corporates have a Dec/Feb year-end, with Q4 buying in Oct–Nov before the December shutdown. (General knowledge, not sourced in this session.)

### Gaps
- No 2025 SA Black Friday electronics-share data, and no Q4 2026 forecasts found.
- No SA data on B2B IT seasonality.

### Overall assessment (market vs company-specific)
- **Market-driven (moderate–strong):** Q2 GDP contraction; BCI 38 (2-year low); CCI negative with affluent consumers most pessimistic; two rate hikes (May and Sep 2026); RAM/laptop price inflation since May–June 2026; Amazon Prime's free next-day delivery since June 2026; pre-Black Friday seasonal lull; pre-election and legal uncertainty in public procurement; chronic municipal late payment.
- **Not explained by the market:** online retail is still growing ~22% in 2026 and retail sales were +3.4% y/y in July 2026. Demand is weaker and shifting to big platforms, but it has not disappeared. Zero sales over several weeks most likely combines a weaker market with company-specific issues (traffic, price competitiveness vs Takealot, Amazon and Evetech, trust signals, payment and delivery options, sales outreach). The client's own analytics should be checked first.
