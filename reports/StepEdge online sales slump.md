# Stop competing on price, start selling relationships

**Recommendation: stop relying on the web shop to bring in sales.** For the next 30 days, put the team's time into three things: the customers StepEdge already has, the quotes already sent, and B-BBEE Level 1 procurement buyers. At the same time, fix the trust and visibility problems that make the shop unconvincing to strangers. The evidence suggests StepEdge has not lost customers so much as lost visibility and price comparisons. Live checks show the sites get **too little traffic to sell from** (well under 100 visits a day on StepEdge and far fewer on IT Net), and Google's own benchmark found most StepEdge prices above market in September. There are **no independent reviews**, and the company names and addresses on the site's terms page conflict with each other. IT Net sells the same catalogue under a second brand. The market is also weaker. GDP shrank in Q2 2026, business confidence is at a two-year low and rates rose twice since May. But online retail is still growing about 22% this year, so the economy does not explain several weeks with no sales at all. The fastest money is in conversations StepEdge can start itself this week: existing hosting and shop customers, and every quote from the last 90 days. Search, reviews and tender work pay off over 30–90 days. **Before acting, the owner should spend one hour pulling traffic and quote numbers (see the last section).** Those numbers decide whether the main problem is "nobody visits" or "visitors don't buy".

## The evidence points to visibility and trust, not just a weak economy

### The market is weaker, but it has not dried up

The economy is clearly working against StepEdge. **GDP fell 0.2% in Q2 2026**, the first drop after six quarters of growth, and investment fell for a second quarter running ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/08/sa-economy-shrinks-in-q2-after-six-quarters-of-growth)). The RMB/BER business confidence index fell from 47 to **38 in Q3 2026**, its lowest since 2024, with "faltering domestic demand" given as a reason ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/print-version/rmbber-business-confidence-index-stabilises-after-second-quarter-setback-2026-09-02)). The Reserve Bank raised rates in May and again on **23 September 2026, taking the repo rate to 7.25%** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/interest-rate/news/586122)). Wealthier consumers, who buy most laptops and IT equipment, "remain deeply pessimistic" ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202609180186.html)). On top of that, rising memory prices have pushed up laptop and component prices since mid-2026 ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-03-23-laptop-blues-sa-cant-hide-from-the-ram-price-spike)). October is also normally a quiet month before Black Friday, which falls on 27 November this year. November now takes **45.9% of combined November–December retail sales** ([Stats SA](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19300)).

Even so, retail sales grew **3.4% year on year in July 2026** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/retail-sales-annual/news/584217)). Online retail is on track for **R159bn in 2026, up 22.5%**, and passed 10% of all retail for the first time ([World Wide Worx](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2026/)). A weaker market explains fewer sales. It does not explain none. The catch is where the growth goes. The share of South Africans who shop online fell from 36.6% to 34.2% ([World Wide Worx](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2026/)), so growth comes from existing shoppers spending more, mostly on large platforms. Takealot is used by **35.3% of online shoppers** ([BusinessDay](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-07-09-takealot-dominates-sa-ecommerce-as-shoppers-favour-chinese-imports/)). **Amazon Prime launched in South Africa on 3 June 2026**, offering free next-day delivery for R59 a month ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/06/09/amazon-prime-launches-in-south-africa-with-streaming-and-free-delivery-for-r59-a-month)). If StepEdge's slump started in mid-2026, the Prime launch is a plausible cause for the loss of online buyers of commodity items like printers and peripherals.

### The real advantage is being underused

StepEdge's strongest asset already ranks in search. Its "B-BBEE Level 1 IT Partner" page appears alongside the procurement-focused competitors ([StepEdge](https://stepedge.co.za/b-bbee-level-1-it-partner/)), and the About page describes the business as **"100% Black & woman-owned"** ([About](https://stepedge.co.za/about/)). Competitors in this space lead with their credentials. AXLTEC puts "B-BBEE Level 1" in its page title and shows "CSD registered, OEM authorised" and a procurement@ email address ([AXLTEC](https://axltec.co.za/)). Black Barter publishes its Central Supplier Database (CSD) number ([Black Barter](https://blackbarter.co/)). Those competitors are based in Soweto and Pretoria. StepEdge is in Edenvale, close to Ekurhuleni's corporate and industrial buyers. The research found **no competitor that combines** Level 1 status, a local presence, hardware, websites and hosting, managed IT, CCTV and POS. That combination is what StepEdge should sell.

There are two caveats on public-sector work. First, under the 2022 preferential procurement regulations, bids of R50m or less are scored **80 points on price and up to 20 on "specific goals"** ([National Treasury PPR 2022](https://www.treasury.gov.za/legislation/regulations/PREFERENTIAL%20PROCUREMENT%20REGULATIONS,%202022.pdf)). StepEdge's current markups would lose on price even with Level 1 status. Bids need special bid pricing from distributors. Second, **81.2% of municipal invoices were outstanding beyond 90 days** ([PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/40050/)). The Constitutional Court struck down the Public Procurement Act on 17 September 2026 ([Accountability Tracker](https://accountability-tracker.org/anti-corruption-policy-monitor/public-procurement-act/)). Live verification confirmed this against EWN, BusinessDay and a National Treasury statement. The ruling was procedural, the Act never came into force, and the existing procurement rules still apply. Corporate supplier-development (ESD) buyers, national and provincial departments, and SOEs are safer targets for cash flow than municipalities.

## Ranked action plan, fastest revenue first

These figures come from published research. Answering an online lead within **5 minutes instead of 30 made it 21 times more likely to be qualified** ([Chili Piper, citing the Oldroyd/InsideSales study](https://www.chilipiper.com/blog/speed-to-lead-statistics)). That measures qualification, not closed sales. Vendor sources also report that **48% of reps never make a second contact after sending a quote** ([Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/newsletter/when-quotes-go-cold-resurrecting-100k-in-lost-opportunities/)). The average B2B win rate is around **19–21%** ([Qwilr](https://qwilr.com/blog/sales-closing-statistics/)). At those rates, StepEdge needs about **15–20 live quotes to expect 3–5 wins**. The plan below is built to get that many quotes out.

### This week (days 1–7): start conversations with people who already know StepEdge

| # | Action | Expected payoff |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Re-contact every quote sent in the last 90 days** by phone first, then WhatsApp. Offer an updated price or a bundle (device + setup + support). Ask lost buyers why they chose someone else. | Fastest cash, plus the reasons deals were lost |

Turn each problem found into a small fixed-price quote. Messaging existing customers is allowed under **POPIA s69(3)** if it is about similar products and **every message includes an opt-out** ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/legislation-direct-marketing/)). | Small add-on sales within 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | **List hosting and domain renewals due in the next 90 days** and offer a discount for paying a year upfront. | Brings cash forward |

## Conclusion

The slump probably has less to do with StepEdge's market than with how the business shows up online. It runs two thin storefronts, priced above the market, with no reviews, while competing with Takealot, Amazon and specialist retailers on the one thing it cannot win: box price. Its real advantages are a 100% Black, woman-owned Level 1 rating, an Edenvale location, an existing hosting customer base, and the ability to bundle hardware with installation and support. These barely appear online, yet they are exactly what corporate and public buyers will pay a premium for. The quickest recovery is to treat sales as outbound work for the next month: get 15–20 live quotes in front of people who already know the business, and fix the trust problems in parallel.

Two uncertainties could change this order. If traffic data shows healthy visits with few sales, then fixing the checkout, payment options, delivery cost and pricing becomes the top priority ahead of SEO. If traffic has collapsed since June, the Amazon Prime effect and lost search rankings are the main story, and the shop should be cut back to a catalogue that supports quotes rather than one that tries to sell by itself.
